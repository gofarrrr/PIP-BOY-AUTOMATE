# PIP-BOY AI Strategy Flowchart - Complete Copy

This document contains all the text content (copy) that is presented to users when interacting with the AI business strategy decision flowchart.

## DECISION NODES

### Do you move atoms?
**Node ID:** `atoms`

> **ANALYSIS**  
> MARKET CLASSIFICATION: Are you in the bits business or the atoms business? If you physically show up somewhere—fix things, touch people, move objects—you're moving atoms. AI cannot do that part. If your work is files, documents, and digital deliverables, you're in bits territory where AI is commoditizing everything.

> **EVALUATION**  
> Can a customer in another country receive your service? If not, you're in atoms. Plumber, dentist, HVAC technician, moving company—these are atoms. Marketing agency, software consultancy, design firm, content production—these are bits.

> **EXECUTION**  
> First fork: physical vs digital. This determines whether AI is a tailwind or a hurricane.

---

### Can customers easily switch?
**Node ID:** `contestable`

> **ANALYSIS**  
> CONTESTABILITY CHECK: A market is contestable when buyers can easily evaluate alternatives and switch. Digital services are highly contestable—I can hire a marketing agency in Hong Kong tomorrow. Physical services are not—I cannot hire a plumber in Hong Kong.

> **EVALUATION**  
> Two tests: (1) Can your customers easily compare your output to competitors? (2) Is switching low-friction? If yes to both, you're in a contestable market. AI is about to intensify competition dramatically.

> **EXECUTION**  
> Contestability determines whether AI will crush margins or just improve efficiency.

---

### Do you sell cognitive production?
**Node ID:** `layer1`

> **ANALYSIS**  
> VALUE CHAIN LAYER: There are three layers of business work. Layer 1 is tokenizable cognition—drafting, analysis, coding, reports. This is collapsing toward zero cost. Layer 2 is judgment and accountability—someone owns the outcome. Layer 3 is physical execution. If you primarily bill for Layer 1, you're vulnerable.

> **EVALUATION**  
> What % of revenue comes from deliverables (drafts, reports, code) vs. outcomes (decisions, accountability, relationships)? If most billings are tied to producing cognitive work, you're selling Layer 1.

> **EXECUTION**  
> Layer 1 is being commoditized. Layer 2 and 3 are not—yet.

---

### Mid-size with established overhead?
**Node ID:** `midtier`

> **ANALYSIS**  
> SIZE TRAP ANALYSIS: The middle tier is where firms are in trouble. Think the marketing agency with 40 employees, the IT consultancy around 15 years, the design firm known for being reliable. These firms are getting squeezed from both directions—from below by tiny AI-native teams, from above by giants with distribution.

> **EVALUATION**  
> Do you have 20-100 employees? Offices? Legacy costs from a pre-AI era? If yes, you're in the squeeze zone. A team of 3 with good AI tools can produce work nearly indistinguishable from yours.

> **EXECUTION**  
> Mid-tier is the danger zone. You must choose a direction.

---

### Have distribution moats?
**Node ID:** `distribution`

> **ANALYSIS**  
> MOAT ASSESSMENT: Distribution moats include embedded client relationships, bundled offerings, platform status, brand recognition. These are not eroded by AI. If anything, they become more valuable as production commoditizes. Distribution is becoming scarce.

> **EVALUATION**  
> Do clients come to you because they have no alternative? Are you embedded in their workflows? Do you have a platform, ecosystem, contracts with switching costs? If yes, your moat is real.

> **EXECUTION**  
> Distribution protects you. Without it, you're in the commodity business.

---

### Can you cut to a core team?
**Node ID:** `can_lean`

> **ANALYSIS**  
> RADICAL RESTRUCTURING: Path one for mid-tier firms is to get radically lean. Cut headcount, cut overhead, rebuild around a small core team that uses AI to produce at current staff levels. You're racing against 3-person startups—act like it.

> **EVALUATION**  
> Can you cut 60-80% of your team and maintain output quality using AI? Is leadership willing? Do you have the technical capability to rebuild AI-native? Painful but survivable.

> **EXECUTION**  
> Getting lean is brutal but viable. The alternative may be worse.

---

### Can you shift to selling judgment?
**Node ID:** `can_pivot`

> **ANALYSIS**  
> VALUE STACK SHIFT: Path two is moving up the stack. Stop selling Layer 1 cognitive production. Start selling Layer 2 judgment, accountability, quality assurance. This means changing what you charge for—not deliverables, but outcomes.

> **EVALUATION**  
> Do you have senior people who can credibly sell judgment and taste? Can you restructure pricing from deliverable-based to outcome-based? AI investments should support senior judgment work, not junior production volume.

> **EXECUTION**  
> Moving up requires repositioning, not just tooling.

---

## OUTCOME NODES

### EFFICIENCY PLAY
**Node ID:** `efficiency`

> **ANALYSIS**  
> TAILWIND POSITION: AI is your friend. It lowers administrative burdens, automates scheduling, dispatch, invoicing, customer communications. It does NOT expose you to brutal competition because your market isn't contestable. You're protected by atoms.

> **EVALUATION**  
> Focus AI investment on: (1) Back-office automation. (2) Customer experience—every call answered, quotes instant. (3) Don't mistake efficiency tools for strategic transformation. You're a plumber. Just do better plumbing.

> **EXECUTION**  
> Don't over-transform. AI is a tool here, not a strategy.

---

### GET LEAN
**Node ID:** `get_lean`

> **ANALYSIS**  
> SURVIVAL MODE: You're racing to the bottom—but owning that race. Cut to a 3-10 person core. Every person AI-fluent. Every deliverable AI-maximized. Your cost structure must match the startups eating your lunch.

> **EVALUATION**  
> Execution: (1) Identify your 10% who can work AI-native. (2) Restructure around them. (3) Automate or eliminate everything else. (4) Price aggressively. (5) Win on speed and cost. Half-lean is just dying slowly.

> **EXECUTION**  
> If you can't beat the lean players, become one. No middle ground.

---

### MOVE UP STACK
**Node ID:** `move_up`

> **ANALYSIS**  
> DIFFERENTIATION PLAY: Compete on judgment, not production. Your value shifts from 'we make things' to 'we ensure things are right.' This is the accountability layer—someone has to sign off, own the outcome, take liability.

> **EVALUATION**  
> Execution: (1) Value-based pricing. (2) Senior people doing judgment work. (3) Stop hiring junior producers—AI replaced them. (4) Build reputation around quality assurance, not volume.

> **EXECUTION**  
> Sell taste, not tokens. Own outcomes, not outputs.

---

### BUILD MOATS
**Node ID:** `build_moats`

> **ANALYSIS**  
> STARTUP SURVIVAL: Pure cognitive production is a depreciating asset. If your value prop is 'we use AI to produce X cheaper,' you're in the commodity business. Every AI-native startup makes the same claim. Margins will compress.

> **EVALUATION**  
> Build defensibility: (1) Distribution—embed in workflows, create switching costs. (2) Second-layer bottlenecks—compliance, audit, human-in-loop review. (3) Accountability wrappers around AI. (4) Workflow orchestration that makes you sticky.

> **EXECUTION**  
> Build what's hard to copy. Production isn't it.

---

### INVEST IN TALENT
**Node ID:** `invest_talent`

> **ANALYSIS**  
> GIANT'S GAME: Your moat protects you. Embedded relationships, platform status, brand—not eroded by AI. The threat is internal: startups will hire away your best people with speed and upside you can't match.

> **EVALUATION**  
> AI investment = talent investment: (1) Give teams AI-native tools. (2) Enable internal innovation at startup speed. (3) Convince top talent they have a future here. The slow death is ankle biters stealing your best people.

> **EXECUTION**  
> Risk is squandering advantage through stagnation. Move faster than comfortable.

---

### ⚠️ DEATH TRAP
**Node ID:** `death_trap`

> **ANALYSIS**  
> WARNING: You're stuck in the middle with no viable path. Can't get lean (unwilling or unable to cut), can't move up (no judgment to sell), and you're selling commoditized work in a contestable market. This kills mid-tier firms.

> **EVALUATION**  
> The death trap is investing in AI to make your current model 20% more efficient. That's not enough to compete with 3-person teams. You're dying slower. If you can't choose lean or up-stack, consider exit, merger, or dramatic niche-down.

> **EXECUTION**  
> The middle is not a strategy. Choose a direction or the market will choose for you.
