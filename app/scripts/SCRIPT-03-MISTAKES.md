### Mistake 1: One-Sided Direction
AI adoption fails when it's purely top-down OR purely bottoms-up.

**Top-down only:** Leaders mandate AI without understanding day-to-day realities. "Just use AI" without time or training allocated.

**Bottoms-up only:** Enthusiastic employees build demos that never reach production because no one owns productization. Innovation as a "side gig."

[DOPAMINE_TRIGGER]
The fix? **Bidirectional.** Leadership provides vision and resources. Employees provide ground truth and adoption. Both must be in the loop from the start.

### Mistake 2: No Clear Governance
"Everyone should work on AI" often means "no one is responsible for AI."

Warning signs: Peers doing the same work don't know what's permitted. When asked "who owns AI?"—people say "the CEO" because they don't know. Zero tools approved after 2+ years.

**The fix:** A dedicated AI team or council—not 10% of someone's time. Written AI policy. Central repository for approved tools.

### Mistake 3: Unrealistic Expectations
The gap between demo and production is often 10x.

A working prototype in days becomes a quarter of work for enterprise deployment. One company winced at "one mistake per week"—that's unrealistic for current AI.

[PATTERN_INTERRUPT]
[VISUAL_CUE: Show "Demo vs Production" timeline comparison]

**The fix:** Plan 10x buffer from demo to production. Set explicit error tolerance BEFORE deployment. Budget training time—few hours minimum per tool.

### Mistake 4: Data Inaccessibility
This is the #1 blocker across all my interviews—even ahead of skills and budget.

Internal data is the ONLY thing that differentiates successful AI adoption. But most organizations have documents scattered across dozens of places, trapped in email, or only in "Bob's head."

One company spent a QUARTER just collecting documents—the actual AI system took two weeks.

**The fix:** Invest in data infrastructure NOW. Consolidate documents. Interview "Bob" and document what only he knows. Build a RAG system. This is the best use of time while waiting for agents to mature.

### Mistake 5: Poor Vendor Strategy
Both extremes fail.

Some organizations build everything in-house, reinventing what vendors already solved better. Others buy everything vendors promise, spending thousands on tools that don't deliver.

**The fix:** Check if capability exists before building. Interview vendor customers, not just sales. Run parallel pilots, not sequential. Get super-users involved in vendor selection.

## SEGMENT 3: THE ORGANIZATIONAL TRAPS (3:30-5:00)

[APPLICATION]

### Mistake 6: Information Silos
Even small companies show employees unaware of what peers are doing with AI—what's permitted, what's already built, what's working.

Large organizations are worse. Silos create duplicated work and leave value on the table.

**The fix:** One place for AI communication—can be as simple as a Slack channel. Publicly celebrate employees who share AI wins. Connect practitioners across teams. Positive reinforcement beats mandates.

### Mistake 7: Waiting for Perfect Tech
"Let's wait for the tech to mature" sounds rational—but is often fatal.

There's a steep learning curve: infrastructure, culture, skills, vendor relationships. Waiting 6-12 months means losing experimentation time competitors won't wait for.

[DOPAMINE_TRIGGER]
Here's the reality: Capabilities are doubling every 7 months. The right lesson? Build readiness NOW—data, governance, skills, culture—so you're ready when capabilities arrive.

**The fix:** Don't wait for perfect tech. Use the time to build infrastructure. Even wrong choices teach. Doing something beats doing nothing.

## BONUS (5:00-5:30)

[PAYOFF]
Here's what most organizations miss: These 7 mistakes compound.

No governance leads to poor vendor decisions. Poor data access makes unrealistic expectations inevitable. Silos prevent learning from mistakes.

**The diagnostic sequence matters:**
1. Alignment (top + bottom)
2. Governance (who owns AI)
3. Expectations (realistic timelines)
4. Data (accessible information)
5. Vendors (build vs buy)
6. Sharing (cross-team communication)
7. Urgency (act now, not later)

Fix them in order—each unlocks the next.
