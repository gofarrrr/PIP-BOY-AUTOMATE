## SEGMENT 1: THE FRAMEWORK (0:30-2:00)

[CONCEPT]
Here's the truth nobody tells you: **frequency is not the right starting point.**

Think of it like this. Your car needs an oil change every 3,000 miles. That's low frequency. But you don't do it yourself—you automate it by scheduling at a shop. Meanwhile, you drive every day—high frequency—but you'd never automate that.

The difference? **Complexity and judgment.**

[VISUAL_CUE: Show decision tree starting with "Do I do this often?"]

The framework has 7 decision points:

1. **Frequency** — Do I do this often?
2. **Enjoyment** — Do I actually like doing it?
3. **Augmentation value** — Could a tool help without full automation?
4. **Complexity** — Is the logic simple or messy?
5. **Process clarity** — Do I know the exact steps?
6. **Success criteria** — Can I measure when it's done right?
7. **Risk** — What happens if it breaks?

[DOPAMINE_TRIGGER]
Here's the surprise: Most tasks that feel automatable fail at question 5 or 6. You THINK you know the steps—but can you write them so a stranger could follow without questions?

## SEGMENT 2: THE THREE OUTCOMES (2:00-3:30)

[EXAMPLE]
Every task lands in one of three buckets:

### AUTOMATE
Perfect for: high frequency, simple logic, clear success criteria, low risk.
Examples: invoice sending, data backup, standard email responses.

The machine works while you sleep. But reaching "automate" doesn't mean you're done—you still need systems connected, team ready, and realistic ROI.

[PATTERN_INTERRUPT]
[VISUAL_CUE: Show "Build vs Buy" comparison]

Pro tip: Before building automation yourself, check if a provider already does this at scale. Their bulk API deals often cost LESS than your DIY solution—plus they handle maintenance.

### AUGMENT
This is the "power armor" zone. You're the pilot; tech is the suit.

[VISUAL_CUE: Show augmentation spectrum]

There are FOUR levels of augmentation:
- **Information**: Dashboards and summaries—tech gathers, you decide
- **Analysis**: Pattern detection—tech highlights, you investigate  
- **Recommendations**: AI suggests actions—you approve or override
- **Hybrid**: Automation handles routine, escalates edge cases to you

Best for complex, high-value work requiring human oversight.

### DO IT YOURSELF
Sometimes the old ways are best. Don't overengineer a sandwich.

If the task is rare, tools are expensive, or the process keeps changing—manual work is the rational choice. No shame in DIY.

## SEGMENT 3: THE HIDDEN TRAPS (3:30-5:00)

[APPLICATION]
Here are the 5 steps to evaluate ANY task:

**Step 1: Track your time for one week.**
You'll be shocked where hours actually go. Time wasters hide in plain sight.

**Step 2: Ask "Do I know the EXACT steps?"**
Not "roughly what I do"—the EXACT recipe a stranger could follow. This is the #1 blocker teams hit. If you can't explain it, you can't delegate it.

**Step 3: Check your data accessibility.**
Simple logic with data trapped in emails is STILL complex. Can a script access what it needs, or is someone copy-pasting?

**Step 4: Define success criteria BEFORE you build.**
"Write a good email" is vague. "Send the PDF invoice" is clear. If you can't measure success, you can't automate reliably.

**Step 5: Run a pilot before you commit.**
One department, one workflow, one week. Prove it works, then scale. Skipping the pilot is how most automation projects fail.

[DOPAMINE_TRIGGER]
The accuracy trap: 95% accuracy sounds amazing until you realize that's 1 in 20 failures. For payroll or invoices? Unacceptable. Use code for precision tasks—not AI.

## BONUS (5:00-5:30)

[PAYOFF]
Here's what most people miss: **Risk isn't just technical.**

Even low-risk automation fails if leadership doesn't support it or the team resists change. Before you build anything, ask:
- Does the organization have appetite for this change?
- Will the team embrace or sabotage it?

Organizational risk is silent and deadly.
