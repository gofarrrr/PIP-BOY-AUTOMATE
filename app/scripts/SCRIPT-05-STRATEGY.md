## SEGMENT 1: THE FIRST QUESTION (0:30-2:00)

[CONCEPT]
Here's the question: **Do you move atoms?**

Are you in the bits business or the atoms business? If you physically show up somewhere—fix things, touch people, move objects—you're moving atoms. AI cannot do that part.

If your work is files, documents, and digital deliverables—you're in bits territory. And that's where AI is commoditizing everything.

[VISUAL_CUE: Two-column: "Atoms Business" vs "Bits Business"]

**The test:** Can a customer in another country receive your service? If not, you're in atoms.

Plumber, dentist, HVAC technician, moving company—atoms.
Marketing agency, software consultancy, design firm—bits.

[DOPAMINE_TRIGGER]
If you're in atoms, AI is a tailwind. It lowers administrative burdens, automates scheduling, improves customer experience. It does NOT expose you to brutal competition because your market isn't contestable.

But if you're in bits? That's where it gets dangerous.

## SEGMENT 2: THE THREE LAYERS (2:00-3:30)

[EXAMPLE]
There are three layers of business work—and AI is eating them differently.

**Layer 1: Tokenizable Cognition**
Drafting, analysis, coding, reports. This is collapsing toward ZERO cost. A 3-person team with AI can now produce work that used to require 40 people.

**Layer 2: Judgment and Accountability**
Someone owns the outcome. Signs off. Takes liability. This layer is NOT being commoditized—yet.

**Layer 3: Physical Execution**
Showing up, touching things, moving atoms. Protected.

[PATTERN_INTERRUPT]
[VISUAL_CUE: Show value chain pyramid with Layer 1 at bottom (red), Layer 2 in middle (yellow), Layer 3 at top (green)]

Here's the problem: Most mid-size businesses primarily bill for Layer 1 work.

That marketing agency with 40 employees? The IT consultancy around 15 years? The design firm known for being reliable?

They're getting squeezed from BOTH directions. From below by tiny AI-native teams. From above by giants with distribution.

---

## SEGMENT 3: THE STRATEGY PATHS (3:30-5:00)

[APPLICATION]
If you're in the squeeze zone, you have TWO paths. Only two.

### Path 1: Get Radically Lean

Cut to a 3-10 person core. Every person AI-fluent. Every deliverable AI-maximized. Your cost structure must match the startups eating your lunch.

**Execution:**
1. Identify your 10% who can work AI-native
2. Restructure around them
3. Automate or eliminate everything else
4. Price aggressively
5. Win on speed and cost

[VISUAL_CUE: Show "Lean vs. Scale" decision fork]

Half-lean is just dying slowly. If you can't beat the lean players, become one.

### Path 2: Move Up the Stack

Compete on judgment, not production. Your value shifts from "we make things" to "we ensure things are right."

**Execution:**
1. Value-based pricing
2. Senior people doing judgment work
3. Stop hiring junior producers—AI replaced them
4. Build reputation around quality assurance, not volume

[DOPAMINE_TRIGGER]
The secret? Sell taste, not tokens. Own outcomes, not outputs.

### The Death Trap

What if you can't get lean AND can't move up?

**This kills mid-tier firms.** Investing in AI to make your current model 20% more efficient is NOT enough to compete. You're dying slower.

If you can't choose lean or up-stack—consider exit, merger, or dramatic niche-down.

The middle is not a strategy. Choose a direction or the market will choose for you.

## BONUS (5:00-5:30)

[PAYOFF]
One more thing: Even if you're protected, there's internal risk.

If you have distribution moats—embedded relationships, bundled offerings, platform status—you're safe from competition. AI can't erode those.

But startups will hire away your best people with speed and upside you can't match. The slow death is ankle biters stealing your talent.

**The fix:** Give teams AI-native tools. Enable internal innovation at startup speed. Convince top talent they have a future here.
